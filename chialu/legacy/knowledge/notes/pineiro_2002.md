## summary
The paper combines a second-degree minimax seed with one modified Goldschmidt iteration for fp64 reciprocal/division/square root/inverse square root. Two unfolded architectures provide either reciprocal/division or all four operations, with shared arithmetic blocks and results accurate to 1 ulp. # pp.1377-1387

## families
### goldschmidt  (role: extends)
mechanism: A second-degree minimax approximation supplies a 30-bit reciprocal seed or 29-bit inverse-square-root seed. The modified iteration computes G, the residual V, and Z through multiplications/multiply-adds; reciprocal/division compute G and V in parallel, while square root/inverse square root compute G before V. Shared multiplexed blocks implement all four operations, and an optional single multiply-add unit can compute G/V/Z sequentially. # pp.1380-1386
choices:
  iterations: 1 [outside domain]   # pp.1380-1381
new_choices:
  iteration_form: reordered_modified_single_iteration — the operation-specific recurrence uses the accurate seed to finish in one iteration   # pp.1380-1381
  operation_set: reciprocal_division | reciprocal_division_sqrt_inverse_sqrt — selects between the two unfolded architectures   # pp.1377-1378
slots:
  seed: poly_seed [degree=2, input_bits=9, function=both]   # pp.1378-1379

errors_and_checks: The reciprocal seed error is less than 2^-30, and the inverse-square-root seed error is less than 2^-29. The final result is guaranteed accurate to 1 ulp. Exactly rounded IEEE results require a remainder calculation such as rem = X * Z - Y for division or rem = Z * Z - X for square root. # pp.1380-1381
conditions: One iteration suffices because the initial approximation has about 30 bits of precision. The complete architecture is slower than the reciprocal/division-only architecture because V follows G on its critical path. The estimates exclude interconnections/remainder computation/final exact rounding and use a rough logic-block model rather than a fabricated implementation. # pp.1380, 1383-1386

### poly_seed  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The input significand is partitioned into X1/X2/X3, and each X1 interval uses C0 + C1 X2 + C2 X2^2 with minimax coefficients generated by the Remez algorithm. Three coefficient tables feed a specialized truncated squarer and a fused multioperand carry-save accumulation tree. Reciprocal and inverse-square-root modes replicate only coefficient tables while sharing the squarer/tree. # pp.1378-1379
choices:
  input_bits: 9   # p.1379
  output_bits: 30 (reciprocal); 29 (recip_sqrt)   # p.1379
  function: both   # pp.1378-1379
  degree: 2   # pp.1378-1379
new_choices:
  evaluator_structure: specialized_squarer_fused_accumulation_tree — identifies the shared arithmetic used to evaluate the polynomial   # pp.1378-1379
  per_function_coefficient_tables: true — reciprocal/inverse-square-root coefficients are separate while the evaluator is shared   # pp.1379, 1382
slots:
  none
parameters: m1 = 9; m2 = 33; X2 wordlength = 24 bits; X2^2 wordlength = 16 bits; reciprocal coefficient widths C0/C1/C2 = 30/20/12 bits; inverse-square-root widths = 29/18/10 bits; 21 accumulated terms; four carry-save levels   # p.1379

## open_questions

* The proposed units guarantee 1 ulp rather than exact rounding, and the optional remainder hardware is not included in the reported estimates. # pp.1381, 1384
