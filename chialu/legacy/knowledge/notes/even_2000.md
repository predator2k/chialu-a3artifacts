## summary
The paper proposes the ES injection-based algorithm for rounding a carry-save encoded floating-point product and compares it with optimized QTF and YZ algorithms (p.1). The ES algorithm produces a rounded double-precision product in 12 logic levels when its injection is included during partial-product reduction, compared with 14 logic levels for QTF and 16 for YZ (p.8).

## families
### round_fused_in_reduction  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The ES algorithm adds an injection determined only by the rounding mode during partial-product reduction, so the resulting SUM/CARRY strings encode EXACT + INJ. A half-adder line partitions the high product, a compound adder computes sum and sum+1, and parallel overflow/no-overflow paths normalize and select the result. Separate logic corrects the least-significant bit when RNE differs from RNU on a tie (pp.2-4).
choices:
  none
new_choices:
  injection_timing: {partial_product_reduction, after_reduction} — determines whether the injection is absorbed into the reduction tree or added by a later carry-save step (p.8)
slots:
  round: injection   # pp.2-4
parameters: 53-bit normalized significands; product range [1,4); four IEEE-754-1985 rounding modes reduced to RZ/RNE/RI; dCLA = 8 logic levels; II UNKNOWN (pp.1-4)
results:

| latency | 14 | logic levels | UNKNOWN; year 2000 | ES with early INJ: 12 logic levels | INJ is added after partial-product reduction; double precision | p.8 |
errors_and_checks: The algorithm supports all four IEEE-754-1985 rounding modes for normalized products and explicitly corrects RNE/RNU tie discrepancies; no numerical error is permitted or reported (pp.2-4). Denormal operands and special values are excluded from the analyzed datapath (p.1).
conditions: The 12-level result requires the injection to be available during partial-product reduction (p.8). A late injection adds a full-adder delay of 2 logic levels (p.8). Any reduction-tree slowdown depends on significand length and adder-tree organization (p.8).
evidence: §3; Fig. 1; equations (1)-(2); delay analysis in §3.4; summary in §6 (pp.2-4, 7-8)

### sig_mul_then_round  (role: compares)
mechanism: The optimized QTF algorithm injects a prediction bit based on the rounding mode and SUM[53]/CARRY[53], then uses half-adder lines, a two-way compound adder, increment-decision logic, normalization, and RNE least-significant-bit correction. The YZ algorithm keeps a three-digit carry-save buffer between its upper and lower partitions, uses customary rounding decisions and small conditional-sum additions, and selects normalized sum/sum+1 candidates (pp.4-7).
choices:
  none
new_choices:
  rounding_algorithm: {QTF, YZ} — selects the prediction-based Quach-derived organization or the Yu-Zyner rounding-table organization (pp.4-7)
  post_normalization_position: {before_round_selection, after_round_selection} — early normalization removes one logic level from the QTF path at the cost of another shifter (pp.6-7)
slots:
  round: compound_adder_select   # pp.4-7
parameters: 53-bit normalized significands; double precision; QTF uses a 53-bit compound adder; YZ uses a 52-bit compound adder and conditional 3-bit/2-bit additions; II UNKNOWN (pp.4-7)
results:

errors_and_checks: Both algorithms implement all four IEEE-754-1985 rounding modes for normalized products; no numerical error is permitted or reported (pp.4-8). The paper states that YZ is easiest to prove correct and QTF has the most intricate rounding-decision logic (p.8).
conditions: QTF and YZ do not require an injection during partial-product reduction (p.8). QTF reaches 14 logic levels only when post-normalization precedes round selection (p.7). ## space_gaps
* The `round_fused_in_reduction` family lacks an `injection_timing` choice for injection during or after partial-product reduction (p.8).
* The `injection` rounding-slot value lacks a declared family describing the injected constant, overflow correction, and RNE tie repair (pp.2-4).
* The `compound_adder_select` rounding-slot value lacks a declared family describing sum/sum+1 generation, normalization position, and increment selection (pp.3-7).

## open_questions
* The fabrication technology/device is not reported because the comparison uses technology-independent logic levels.
* The exact parallel-prefix topology of each compound adder is not fixed; Brent-Kung is cited only as an implementation that satisfies the assumed early-MSB timing (p.4).
* The QTF and YZ results describe speed-optimized reconstructions rather than unchanged implementations from the cited source papers (pp.4, 6).
