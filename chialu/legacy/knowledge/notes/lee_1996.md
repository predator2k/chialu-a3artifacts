## summary
MAX-2 adds packed 16-bit integer arithmetic, shifts, and rearrangement operations to the 64-bit PA-RISC 2.0 architecture by modifying existing integer ALU and shift-merge resources (pp.51-54). Each instruction operates on four contiguous subwords, and all listed MAX-2 instructions complete in one cycle (p.52). MAX-2 occupies less than 0.1 percent of PA-8000 silicon area and causes no processor cycle-time impact (p.59).

## new_families
### shared_segmented_simd_datapath  (domain: shift: sub-word SIMD, closest: partitioned_carry_chain, why_not: partitioned_carry_chain covers only addition, while MAX-2 segments and reuses the ALU/shifter/rearrangement datapath)
mechanism: MAX-2 packs four contiguous 16-bit subwords into a 64-bit word. Existing integer ALUs execute packed arithmetic, while modified shift-merge units execute boundary-blocked shifts, two-source mix operations, and one-source permutations. HSHR/HSHL prevent bits leaving one subword from entering an adjacent subword. MIX interleaves alternate subwords from two registers, and PERMH selects each destination subword from any source subword with repetition permitted (pp.52-54).

## space_gaps
* `partitioned_carry_chain` lacks an arithmetic-mode choice for modulo/signed-saturation/unsigned-saturation packed operations (pp.52-53).
* The shifter vocabulary lacks a subword-boundary-blocking choice for a word-wide shifter reused as independent packed lanes (p.53).
* The sub-word SIMD vocabulary lacks the shared-segmented organization used by MAX-2, which modifies existing ALU/SMU resources rather than replicating lane units (pp.54, 59).
* The rearrangement vocabulary lacks MAX-2’s combination of two-source alternate-subword mix and indexed one-source permutation with repetition (p.54).

## open_questions
* The document does not specify the gate-level carry-boundary mechanism used by HADD/HSUB.
* The document does not specify the internal mux topology or radix of the existing 64-bit shifter and shift-merge units.
* The document does not separate the silicon-area contributions of arithmetic, shifting, mix, and permute support.
