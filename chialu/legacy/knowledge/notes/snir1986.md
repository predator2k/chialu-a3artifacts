## summary
The paper proves a depth-size lower bound for general parallel-prefix circuits and constructs optimal circuits across a stated depth range (pp.185-196). The paper also derives bounds for carry-lookahead circuits and bounded-width prefix computation (pp.189-200).

## families
### parallel_prefix  (role: analyzes)
mechanism: A prefix circuit computes every prefix of an associative operation using unary and binary nodes. The lower bound applies even when each node may perform an arbitrary binary operation. Conservative constructions compose a sequential circuit S_n with a compressed, unrolled divide-and-conquer circuit P_n derived from Brent and Kung. The constructions trade one added node for each unit of depth removed and include schedules with at most w nodes per level. # pp.185-199

### carry_lookahead  (role: analyzes)
mechanism: The circuit receives generate signals g_1,…,g_n, propagate signals p_1,…,p_n, and initial carry g_0. It computes carries using c_i=g_i+p_i·c_{i−1}. An induction specialized to this recurrence produces a stronger depth-size lower bound than the general n-input prefix bound. # pp.189-190,200-201

## space_gaps
* `parallel_prefix.fanout_cap` lacks values for unbounded fanout and fanout of order lg n, which are the model and construction covered by the paper (pp.199-200).
* `parallel_prefix` lacks a computation-width choice for the maximum number w of nodes scheduled at one level (p.196).
* `parallel_prefix.topology` does not distinguish the unrolled/compressed P_n construction or compositions of P_n and S_n (pp.191-195).

## open_questions
* The optimal tradeoff for bounded-fanout circuits remains unsettled (pp.199-200).
* The paper does not consider ⌈lg n⌉ ≤ t < 2⌈lg n⌉−2, and it does not establish whether zero-deficiency circuits exist there (pp.199-200).
* The paper leaves open whether optimal circuits must be conservative and satisfy the stated good-nesting property (p.200).
