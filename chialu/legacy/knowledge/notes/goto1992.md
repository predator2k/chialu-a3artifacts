## summary
The document proposes a 54 x 54-b regularly structured tree multiplier that combines second-order Booth partial-product generation, recurring 7-2 compression blocks, recurring wire shifters, and a final Manchester CPA. The fabricated 0.8-pm CMOS implementation completes multiplication in 13.0 ns and uses 82 500 transistors. # p.1229, p.1233-1235

## families
### booth_recoded_parallel  (role: instantiates)
mechanism: Second-order Booth encoding produces 27 partial-product bits at a bit position, with one additional sign bit. Each P unit generates a partial-product bit from multiplicand bits and Booth signals representing X1, X2, and partial-product negation. A Ph unit corrects the partial-product sign without extending it through the most significant 107th bit. # p.1229, p.1232

### compressor_4_2_tree  (role: extends)
mechanism: The regularly structured tree is basically a Wallace tree whose partial-product generators and adder tree are partitioned into recurring 7D subblocks. A 7D subblock combines seven partial-product-bit generators with a 7-2 compressor. Four contiguous subblocks form the repeatable 7D4 block from 4D/3D units and 4W 4-2 compressors. Recurring wire-shifter patterns connect compression levels, so the repeated structure includes both logic and interblock wiring. Four 4-2 compressors on the critical path correspond to 12 XOR-gate delays. # p.1229-1233

## space_gaps
* compressor_4_2_tree appears as a slot value but lacks declared choices for recurring block granularity, integration of partial-product generation, recurring wiring, and boundary-block variants, which are the document's principal design axes. # p.1229-p.1233
* manchester_carry_chain lacks a value for the document's conflict-free bypass and adding scheme. # p.1233, p.1235

## open_questions
* The document identifies the CPA as a Manchester adder with a new bypass scheme but does not state its chain segmentation. # p.1233
* The 0.5-pm area/delay values are analytical shrink results rather than measurements from the fabricated 0.8-pm chip. # p.1234
