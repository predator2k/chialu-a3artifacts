## summary
Sanity-Check uses spatial and temporal checksums to detect hardware-caused errors in fully-connected and convolutional DNN layers. The checksums can execute in software or through extra systolic-array MAC/accumulator hardware, and the experiments report perfect error-caused misprediction coverage without false alarms. (p.7, p.11–12)

## families
### abft_checksum  (role: extends)
mechanism: Sanity-Check embeds linear invariants in fully-connected and convolutional layers. A spatial checksum adds a neuron or filter whose weights and bias are additive inverses of the existing values, so the corresponding outputs sum to zero. A temporal checksum appends the additive inverse of an input-batch sum and compares the output sum with a predetermined constant. The hardware form adds a systolic-array MAC column, an adder row, sanity input generators, and check accumulators. (p.8–11)

errors_and_checks: A temporal checksum guarantees detection of one input error, while a spatial checksum guarantees detection of one weight/filter error. Spatial checksums detect one bias error, and temporal checksums detect all bias errors. Either checksum detects one output/computation error. Their combination guarantees detection of three or fewer output/computation errors, but correction is disabled because some three-error signatures resemble single-error signatures. (p.10) Experiments inject random bit errors into 16-bit fixed-point inputs/weights/biases/outputs; weight/bias errors persist until refresh, while input/output errors are dynamic. (p.10) Thresholds from 2x to 100x the maximum training-set deviation deliver perfect error-caused misprediction coverage and no false alarms in the reported experiments. (p.11–12) Residue arithmetic introduces rare aliases that may weaken the all-bias-error guarantee. (p.11)
conditions: Spatial checksums do not detect input errors, and temporal checksums do not detect weight/filter errors, so both checksums are required for their combined guarantees. (p.10) The study limits convolution protection to the filter and temporal-batch dimensions because x/y checksums require S or R extra padding rows/columns and multiple vector sums. (p.10) The proofs assume real arithmetic, while implemented floating-point/truncated fixed-point checks require a deviation threshold. (p.11) Temporal checksums reduce throughput by 1/(N+1), while the added hardware removes spatial-check throughput loss. (p.11) Permanent faults beyond checksum violations and fault models beyond bit errors are not experimentally investigated. (p.7)
evidence: §III.A–C and Figs. 3–6 (p.8–10); §IV.A–C and Figs. 7–9 (p.10–11); §V, Fig. 10, and Table I (p.11–12)

## space_gaps
* `abft_checksum.placement` cannot represent a design supplied in both `software` and `array_edge` forms without a multi-value convention. (p.7, p.10–11)
* `abft_checksum` lacks a comparison-threshold choice for tolerating numerical checksum deviations. (p.11–12)
* `abft_checksum` lacks a checksum-arithmetic choice distinguishing native arithmetic from residue arithmetic with aliasing. (p.11)

## open_questions
* Fig. 10 does not tabulate the exact error-coverage percentages for each error multiplicity and threshold.
* The deployed detection threshold within the reported 2x-to-100x range is not fixed.
* The arithmetic-unit area ratio n must be determined for each target architecture.
