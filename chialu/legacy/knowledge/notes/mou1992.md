## summary
The document introduces recursively constructed Overturned-Stairs carry-save adder trees with regular rectangular layouts and speed near the Wallace-tree bound (pp.940-943). The document applies an OS tree to a 16 × 16 2’s-complement parallel multiplier and generalizes the construction to N × N multiplication (pp.944-947).

## new_families
### overturned_stairs_reduction_tree  (domain: mul: integer multipliers, closest: carry_save_array, why_not: OS trees recursively arrange 3-2 CSAs into tree-shaped stairs rather than a regular 2-D array.)
mechanism: An OS tree recursively constructs a body of height j from a body of height j − 1, a branch of height j − 2, and a two-CSA connector. A branch and connector form one stair. Vertical CSA placement with fixed feedthrough channels produces a rectangular bit-slice layout that is replicated across the word. Higher-order trees replace branches with equal-height OS or ZM trees, which increases wiring while reducing tree height. The multiplier instantiation reduces N Baugh-Wooley partial products with an N-input OS tree (pp.941-946).

## space_gaps
* `csa_reduction_tree` appears as a slot value elsewhere in the vocabulary but lacks a defined family with topology/order/feedthrough choices; OS trees provide such a topology (pp.940-943).
* The proposed reduction-tree family needs a `final_cpa` slot: the multiplier requires a carry-look-ahead final adder, although the final adder is not designed in the paper (pp.945-946).
* `baugh_wooley` needs a variant choice: the selected variant generates at most N bits per weight, whereas the alternative generates N + 2 bits at weight 2^(N−1) and is less regular for CSA trees (pp.944-945).

## open_questions
* Table III and the conclusion disagree on the first input count where OS variants miss the Wallace bound: Table III identifies N = 42, while the surrounding comparison text states N = 41; both list N = 61-63 and 89-94 (pp.944, 947).
* The final carry-look-ahead adder’s topology, width, delay, and area remain unspecified because the paper excludes it from the multiplier implementation study (pp.945-947).
