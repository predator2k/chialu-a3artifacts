## summary
Stripes accelerates DNN convolution by feeding neuron bits serially while processing synapses in parallel, so each layer takes P cycles for P-bit neurons (pp.2-3). The evaluated extension to DaDianNao improves performance by 2.24x and energy efficiency by 2.00x on average with estimated 5% area and 12% power overheads (p.4).

## families
### bit_serial_dnn_datapath  (role: proposes)
mechanism: Each neuron is supplied one bit per cycle while each 16-bit synapse is supplied in parallel. AND gates combine the current neuron bits with synapses, 16-input adder trees reduce the terms, and the partial outputs are shifted and accumulated each cycle. The design processes 16 windows in parallel to offset the P-cycle latency of a P-bit neuron and reuses the same synapses throughout those cycles (pp.2-3).

errors_and_checks: Per-layer fixed-point precision is selected to maintain the classification accuracy of the baseline 16-bit implementation; no arithmetic error bound or detection mechanism is reported.   # p.1
conditions: Execution time scales linearly with each layer's neuron precision, with ideal speedup 16/P relative to the 16-bit baseline (p.1). The design relies on convolutional-layer parallelism to process 256 neurons concurrently (p.3). Dispatcher stalls can occur when a neuron chunk spans more memory rows than P (p.3). The evaluation covers convolutional layers and uses estimated area/energy models because a fully synthesized Stripes design was not available (p.4). Fully connected layers require a modification, and signed neuron values require additional hardware (p.4).

## space_gaps
* `bit_serial_dnn_datapath` lacks a choice distinguishing the bit-serial neuron operand from the bit-parallel synapse operand (p.2).
* `bit_serial_dnn_datapath` lacks an explicit shifted-accumulator component slot for the per-cycle reduced sums (pp.2-3).

## open_questions
* The paper does not state the fixed-point encoding or signedness of the evaluated neuron values; signed-neuron support is only identified as requiring additional hardware (p.4).
* The paper does not specify the internal adder-tree family used for the 16-input reductions (p.3).
* The paper reports estimated full-chip area/power rather than a fully synthesized Stripes implementation (p.4).
