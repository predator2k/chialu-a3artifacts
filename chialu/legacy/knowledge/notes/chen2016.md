## summary
The paper proposes unsigned approximate restoring array dividers using approximate subtractor cells, cell replacement, and cell truncation (pp.2-4). The restoring designs reduce power/circuit complexity relative to approximate non-restoring dividers while slightly reducing error, especially for quotient-oriented image processing (pp.5-11).

## families
### approximate_recurrence  (role: proposes)
mechanism: A non-performing restoring array uses EXDCr cells composed of an exact subtractor cell and a 2-to-1 MUX. AXDCr1/AXDCr2/AXDCr3 replace the subtractor with AXSC1/AXSC2/AXSC3. Approximation replaces or removes cells in vertical, horizontal, square, or triangle regions. Truncation passes the removed cell's X input toward the remainder and discards Y. Earlier quotient errors propagate through subsequent subtraction rows, so quotient Q and remainder R have distinct error behavior. (pp.2-4)

errors_and_checks: ED is the absolute accurate/approximate output difference; MED is mean ED; NED is MED divided by maximum possible ED, stated as 2^(N-1)-1. The 8- and 16-bit designs are evaluated exhaustively, while the 32-bit design uses 1 million random input patterns. Q and R are evaluated separately; no maximum-error guarantee or fault-detection contract is reported.   # p.5
conditions: Triangle replacement gives the smallest Q NED and the best image PSNR, while vertical replacement gives the worst image PSNR (pp.5,10). AXDr1 triangle replacement gives the smallest MPP when both accuracy and power matter (p.7). Truncation saves more power at high depth but introduces more error than replacement (pp.5,7). AXSC2-based dividers have the largest Q error, while AXSC1/AXSC3 generally have the smallest NED (p.5). Approximate dividers suit quotient-oriented image processing better than remainder-oriented modulo operations because R depends recursively on Q (pp.10-11).
evidence: §§2.1, 2.3, 3-5; Figs. 4, 8-26 (pp.2-10); conclusion items 1-5 (p.11)

### restoring_nonrestoring  (role: instantiates)
mechanism: The exact base is an unsigned restoring array arranged as non-performing division. Each EXDCr contains an exact subtractor cell and a quotient-controlled 2-to-1 MUX. The restoring arrangement eliminates the remainder-correction circuit required by the compared non-restoring divider. Approximate variants substitute AXSC1/AXSC2/AXSC3 inside the same cell structure.
