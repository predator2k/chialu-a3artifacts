## summary
The paper empirically compares benchmark circuits implemented in a 90 nm Altera Stratix II FPGA and 90 nm standard-cell ASIC technology for area, critical-path delay, and power (pp.21–26). Hard DSP multiplier-accumulator blocks reduce the FPGA/ASIC area gap but provide little average delay benefit for the selected benchmarks (pp.27–30). The work evaluates implementation media rather than proposing an arithmetic-unit microarchitecture.

A 9x9 multiplier is its smallest division; four such multipliers form an 18x18 multiplier, and eight form a 36x36 multiplier. The study compares implementations with these blocks against implementations using programmable logic and against standard-cell ASIC implementations. (p.23)

conditions: Hard DSP blocks primarily improve area efficiency rather than speed for the selected benchmarks (pp.27–30). Fixed DSP placement can erase a multiplier’s speed advantage through additional routing (p.28). The 5x5 and 8x8 multiplications gain little from 9x9 hard multipliers, while the tested 32x32 operations do not fully use hardware designed for 36x36 multiplication (p.28). The area analysis counts only used FPGA resources and ignores device-size granularity and fixed heterogeneous-resource ratios, which favors the FPGA (pp.25, 27). Dynamic-power estimates use either simulation vectors or uniform toggle-rate/static-probability assumptions, and the study reports no reliable static-power comparison (pp.26, 29).
evidence: Table 1 and §4 establish DSP dimensions/inference (p.23); Table 2 and §7.1 report area (p.27); Tables 3–4 and §7.2 report delay (pp.28–29); Table 5 and §7.3 report power (p.29).
