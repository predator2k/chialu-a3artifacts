## summary
The paper proposes fused floating-point add–subtract architectures that generate a sum and difference simultaneously while sharing exponent comparison, significand swap, and alignment logic (p.2286). A dual-path architecture reduces latency, and a balanced two-stage pipeline increases throughput (pp.2287-2291).

## families
### two_path  (role: extends)
mechanism: The far path handles exponent differences greater than 1 and performs addition, subtraction, and rounding in parallel without leading-zero anticipation or large normalization. The close path handles exponent differences of 0 or 1 through three parallel case-specific additions, subtractions, and LZAs. The close path selects a case using the two exponent LSBs and normalizes subtraction results by up to 23 bits (pp.2287-2288).

### parallel_prefix  (role: instantiates)
mechanism: Kogge–Stone parallel-prefix adders implement the significand addition and subtraction operations in the far and close paths. The logarithmic delay growth of the parallel-prefix form limits the fp64 latency increase relative to fp32 (pp.2287, 2290).

## new_families
### fused_fp_add_subtract  (domain: fp: floating-point adders, closest: two_path, why_not: `two_path` does not represent simultaneous A+B and A-B outputs with shared front-end logic)
mechanism: The unit accepts two normalized floating-point operands and produces their sum and difference simultaneously. Exponent comparison, significand swap, and alignment are shared. Separate significand addition and subtraction operations feed output-selection multiplexers controlled by the operand signs. Only the subtraction needs LZA and normalization. The improved version uses far and close paths, and the pipelined version divides the dual-path dataflow into two balanced stages (pp.2285-2290).

## space_gaps
* The `two_path` family needs a choice for simultaneous sum/difference outputs because this design computes both rather than selecting one add/subtract result (pp.2285-2289).
* The floating-point adder vocabulary lacks an `abruptUnderflow` exception-handling choice, which the implementation explicitly supports (pp.2288-2289).
* The rounding slots do not represent the far-path rounded/unrounded parallel results with late selection and the close-path rounding bypass (pp.2287-2288).

## open_questions
* The exact numeric cells of Tables III-V are not present in the supplied document text, so only values stated in surrounding prose are recorded.
* The paper specifies normalized input operands but does not settle whether subnormal inputs are accepted in hardware.
