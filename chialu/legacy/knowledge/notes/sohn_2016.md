## summary
The paper proposes a four-stage fused unit for \(AB \pm CD \pm EF \pm GH\) with one shared alignment/reduction/normalization/rounding path. Direct exponent-difference routing, dual reduction, early normalization, four-input LZA, and compound addition/rounding reduce area/latency/power by about 40% against four discrete multipliers and three adders. The design supports single/double precision and all four IEEE-754 binary rounding modes.

## families
### multi_term_fused_dot  (role: proposes)
mechanism: Four multiplier trees produce sum/carry pairs, which are aligned to the largest product exponent without operand sorting. Duplicated 4:2 CSA trees form opposite-sign candidate pairs, and a significand comparison selects the positive pair. A four-input LZA predicts the normalization shift before the main addition. Early normalization halves the main-addition width and fixes the rounding positions, so a compound adder computes sum/sum+1 while rounding proceeds in parallel. # pp.3-7

### parallel_prefix  (role: instantiates)

## new_families
### leading_zero_anticipation  (domain: fp floating-point adders/dot products, closest: lzd_cell_tree, why_not: it predicts the normalization shift from redundant addends before addition rather than counting zeros in a completed word)
mechanism: The four-input LZA encodes each column sum \(w_i \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}\) as \(p0_i\) through \(p4_i\), derives \(g_i\), \(t_i\), and \(z_i\), and generates an F vector for an LZD. Higher shift bits are generated first to overlap the LZD and normalization shifter. Concurrent correction adds 1 for identified one-bit prediction-error patterns. # p.6
