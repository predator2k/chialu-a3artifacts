## summary
The paper reduces circular CORDIC rotation iterations by representing a known angle with a sparse set of elementary angles whose coefficients are 0, +1, or -1. A greedy O(n^2) recoding algorithm guarantees fewer than n/2 elementary rotations without changing the stated angle-approximation accuracy, and simulations report rotation/scaling iteration counts for n = 16 and n = 32.

## families
### cordic  (role: extends)
mechanism: The angle θ is represented as a linear combination of elementary angles a(i) with coefficients u(i) ∈ {0, ±1}. At each recoding step, the greedy algorithm selects the elementary angle closest to the remaining angle and assigns its coefficient from the residual sign. Only nonzero coefficients require CORDIC shift-and-add rotations. The resulting norm-correction constant is known after recoding, so a modified Booth representation supplies additional signed shift-and-add iterations for scaling. (pp. 99-100)
