## summary
This clause defines the terminology used throughout the standard, including formats, representations, rounding, operations, NaNs, exceptions, and status flags. It defines fusedMultiplyAdd as an unbounded intermediate calculation rounded once to the destination format.

## other_normative
* **Required:** The terms and definitions in 2.1 apply for the purposes of this standard. # p.14
* **Required:** A basic format is one of five representations, comprising three binary and two decimal formats, whose encodings are specified by the standard and which can be used for arithmetic. A conforming implementation implements one or more basic formats. # p.14
* **Required:** A biased exponent is the exponent plus a constant bias chosen to make the biased-exponent range nonnegative. # p.14
* **Required:** A canonical encoding is the preferred encoding of a floating-point representation in a format. # p.14
* **Required:** A cohort contains all representations of a given floating-point number in a given format. −0 and +0 are distinct and belong to different cohorts. # p.14
* **Required:** A computational operation can signal floating-point exceptions, produce floating-point results, or produce integer results rounded to fit destination formats under a rounding-direction rule. Comparisons are computational operations. # p.14
* **Required:** A declet encodes three decimal digits in ten bits using densely-packed decimal. The 1024 possible declets comprise 1000 canonical declets produced by computational operations and 24 non-canonical declets accepted in operands but not produced by computational operations. # p.14
* **Required:** An operation result is defined in terms of the destination format and operand values, including when a language controls the destination of an intermediate calculation. # p.14
* **Required:** For precision `p`, exponent forms `e` and `q` satisfy `e = q + p − 1`; `e` applies when the significand is viewed as an integer digit and fraction field, while `q` applies when the significand is viewed as an integer. # p.15
* **Required:** A floating-point datum is a representable floating-point number or NaN. # p.15
* **Required:** A floating-point number is a representable finite or infinite number that is not a NaN. All floating-point numbers, including zeros and infinities, are signed. # p.15
* **Required:** A finite floating-point representation has a sign, exponent, and significand. Its value is the signed product of the significand and the radix raised to the exponent. A representation may instead denote signed infinity, quiet NaN, or signaling NaN. # p.15
* **Required:** An integer format is outside this standard and represents a subset of the integers, possibly with infinities, NaNs, or negative zeros. # p.15
* **Required:** A format is narrower than another when its set of floating-point numbers is a proper subset of the other format’s set. # p.15
* **Required:** A normal number is a finite non-zero number with magnitude at least `b^emin`, where `b` is the radix. Zero is neither normal nor subnormal. # p.15
* **Required:** A NaN is a symbolic non-number with quiet and signaling representations. A NaN payload is diagnostic information encoded in part of the trailing significand field. # p.15
* **Required:** Precision `p` is the maximum number of significant digits representable in a format or the number of digits to which a result is rounded. # p.16
* **Required:** For a decimal operation with an exact result, the preferred exponent is the exponent `q` that preserves the operands’ quantum. # p.16
* **Required:** The quantum of a finite representation is one unit in the last place of its significand and equals the radix raised to exponent `q` when the significand is viewed as an integer. # p.16
* **Required:** A quiet operation never signals a floating-point exception. # p.16
* **Required:** The radix of a binary or decimal floating-point number is two or ten, respectively. # p.16
* **Required:** A result is the floating-point representation or encoding delivered to the destination. # p.16
* **Required:** A subnormal number is a non-zero number whose magnitude is less than the format’s smallest normal magnitude. A subnormal number does not use the full precision available to normal numbers of that format. # p.16
* **Required:** A supported floating-point format is provided by the programming environment and implemented in conformance with the standard. An integer format is supported when its conversions to and from supported floating-point formats conform to the standard. # p.16
* **Required:** A trailing significand field contains every significand digit except the leading digit; the biased exponent or combination field encodes or implies the leading digit. # p.16
* **Required:** The width of an operation is its destination format and is one of the implementation’s supported formats. # p.16
* **Required:** `LSB`, `MSB`, `NaN`, `qNaN`, and `sNaN` mean least significant bit, most significant bit, not a number, quiet NaN, and signaling NaN, respectively. # p.16

## model_gaps
* The model has one NaN and cannot represent the quiet/signaling distinction or NaN payloads. This gap affects bit-exact NaN encodings/results and signaling-NaN behavior. # p.15
* The model has no exception flags or traps, so it cannot represent invalid-operation signaling or the inexact/underflow/overflow/divideByZero/invalid-operation status flags. This gap does not change an otherwise specified numeric result, but it omits required observable exception state defined elsewhere. # p.14
* The model does not represent decimal floating-point formats, decimal cohorts, declets, preferred exponents, or decimal quantum. This gap affects bit-exact decimal encodings and results. # p.14
* The model does not represent language-defined applicable attributes, dynamic modes, or destination-selection methods. This gap can affect bit-exact results when those mechanisms select a rounding direction or destination format. # p.14

## open_questions
* This clause does not specify the available rounding directions or their tie rules; the implementer must obtain them from the rounding clause.
* This clause does not identify the exact operations covered by “Most operations” in the NaN definition; the implementer must obtain operation-specific NaN behavior from later clauses.
* This clause does not specify format widths, exponent ranges, biases, precisions, or encodings; the implementer must obtain those parameters from the format clause.
