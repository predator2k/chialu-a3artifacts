## summary
The paper proposes a family of parallel squarers that recursively divides the operand and uses optimized primitive squarers as base cases, which reduces gates and critical-path length relative to Dadda-style parallel squaring (pp.909–910). A fabricated 8-b design performs one squaring operation per clock cycle at 24 MOPS in 2-µm CMOS (p.911).

## families
### squarer  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The divide-and-conquer recursion partitions an 8-b operand into subvectors, computes smaller squares and cross-products, and combines them through carry-save and carry-propagate addition. Optimized primitive squarers terminate the recursion before 1-b squaring. The 4-b primitive gives the smallest analyzed gate count and critical path for an 8-b squarer, while the fabricated design uses available 2-b library primitives (pp.909–911).

conditions: The 4-b primitive is optimal among the analyzed 8-b structures, but the chip uses the nearly optimal 2-b decomposition because ACME lacked a 4-b primitive-squarer library cell (pp.910–911). The method targets short input words, and the authors estimate that parallel divide-and-conquer squarers may remain cost-effective through 16 b with preferred 3-, 4-, or 6-b primitives (p.912). The lower-power claim is a transition-density/glitch argument rather than a measured chip result (pp.911–912).
evidence: Section II, equations (1)–(3), Figs. 1–3 (pp.909–910); Section III, Figs. 4–7 (pp.910–912); Section IV (p.912).

## space_gaps
* `squarer` lacks a `primitive_squarer_size` choice for the recursion base-case width, which determines the reported gate-count and critical-path tradeoff (pp.910–912).
* `squarer` lacks a `final_adder` slot; the fabricated design uses `ripple_carry` after its carry-save stage (p.911).

## open_questions
* The paper does not report transistor count, measured power, or delay in time units, so the gate-delay comparisons and lower-power argument cannot be converted into those metrics (pp.910–912).
