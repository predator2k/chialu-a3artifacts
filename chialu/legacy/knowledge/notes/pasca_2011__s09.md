## summary
The chapter classifies square-root implementations as digit-recurrence or multiplication-based algorithms and compares a classical FloPoCo recurrence with a table-driven polynomial implementation. The polynomial implementation reduces latency but increases resource use at large precision, while square-and-compare correct rounding adds substantial area/latency over faithful rounding. The chapter advocates faithful rounding for FPGA approximation-based square roots.   # p.115, p.116, p.122

## new_families
### sqrt_square_compare_rounding  (domain: div: multiplicative final rounding, closest: back_multiply_remainder, why_not: the decision squares a square-root candidate rather than multiplying a quotient by its divisor)
mechanism: A faithfully rounded square root re at precision wF+1 is either representable at wF or lies at a midpoint. When re is a midpoint, comparing re² with x selects truncation or upward correction. Only square-product bits needed to determine the comparison need be computed.   # p.117, p.118
choices: candidate_guard_bits: {1}; comparison: {full_square, partial_low_square}; correction: {truncate, add_2^-wF-1_then_truncate}   # p.117, p.118
