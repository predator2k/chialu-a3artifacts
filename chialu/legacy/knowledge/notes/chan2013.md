## summary
The document proposes lookup-table characterization and regression-based composition for estimating errors at internal nodes and outputs of circuits containing approximate adders. The method models intrinsic/propagated errors as functions of input standard deviations and hardware configuration, then traverses the circuit topology to compose six error metrics. Experiments cover an FIR filter, 280 MAC circuits, and randomly generated circuits with up to 30 nodes.

## families
### error_analysis_quality  (role: proposes)
mechanism: Pre-characterized EMin tables map input standard deviations and approximate-adder configurations to intrinsic error metrics, while STDZ tables propagate value-distribution statistics through the circuit topology. Topological traversal annotates each node before regression equations combine intrinsic EMin with propagated EMA and EMB. ER composition multiplies fitted pass-rate terms; ES, ARES, MSE, SNR, and MAXE use a fitted linear expression. Monte Carlo simulation supplies reference values and training observations. The model is developed for approximate adders; multiplication and other operations remain future work. # pp.50-51

errors_and_checks: ER, ES, ARES, MSE, SNR, and MAXE are estimated against Monte Carlo simulation using relative and absolute estimation-inaccuracy measures. No formal accuracy bound, hardware fault model, detection coverage, false-alarm behavior, or alias rate is provided.   # pp.48,51-52
conditions: Gaussian inputs with zero mean and varying standard deviations are used for characterization; the motivating study assumes equal operand standard deviations. Constant multipliers in FIR/MAC tests are accurate. ARES and SNR are unreliable near zero-valued data because their relative-error definitions and limited LUT grids amplify estimation error. Linear regression can worsen relative inaccuracy for ES/MSE/MAXE, and MAXE outliers weaken the fit. The composition rules are developed for adders rather than multipliers or other arithmetic modules.   # pp.50-53

## open_questions
* The document leaves the regression composition rules for approximate multipliers and other arithmetic operations as future work.   # pp.50,53
* The document does not establish how non-Gaussian or changing application distributions should be characterized; distribution tracking, runtime reconfiguration, and basis-distribution decomposition remain future work.   # p.53
* The document does not resolve accurate composition of ARES/SNR or regression treatment of MAXE outliers.   # pp.51-53
