## summary
The document proposes checksum-based algorithm-based error detection for GPU CNN convolutions, including filter-only (FC), input-only (IC), and filter-and-input (FIC) variants (pp.3-4). The implementations preserve exact int8 checking through widened arithmetic and reduce overhead through framework/kernel fusion (pp.5-7). FIC incurs 6-23% runtime overhead and detects all observed convolution corruptions in the reported experiments (pp.9,11).

## families
### abft_checksum  (role: extends)
mechanism: The schemes verify a reduced convolution output computed from input checksums through a different sequence of integer sums/products. FC sums filters element-wise, convolves the checksum filter, and compares its output with channel-reduced output feature maps. IC reduces input feature maps and compares per-filter products with reduced outputs. FIC computes filter/input checksums, takes their dot product, reduces the complete convolution output, and compares the two values for equality. Filter checksums are generated offline; input/output checksums and verification execute online. # pp.3-4

errors_and_checks: Exact integer equality is used after worst-case width sizing; int64 suffices for the evaluated networks, while modular arithmetic would bound precision with unquantified coverage loss (pp.5-6). FC detects all injected single-bit errors in nonzero filters/output feature maps but none in input feature maps; FIC detects injected errors in inputs/filters/outputs (p.11). FIC observed no SDCs with on-chip ECC off and detected all observed SDCs with ECC on, although exposure counts and confidence bounds are not printed (p.11). fp16 checking requires a threshold and may produce false positives because convolution rounding error is not bounded (p.11).
conditions: FC omits input-feature-map storage/transport protection, while IC omits filter protection and FIC protects both (pp.3-4). IC is not evaluated further because its runtime is expected to resemble FIC while its protection is lower (p.4). FC can benefit from pruning that leaves room for checksum filters, but crossing a convolution/GEMM tile boundary can raise runtime by up to 2× (pp.9-10). FIC avoids a larger convolution but requires online input checksum generation (p.4). Activation/pooling protection requires duplication or another mechanism because checksum linearity does not cover nonlinear layers (pp.2,10).
evidence: §§3-4; Tables 1-2; Figures 2,5-13; §§6.1-6.4 and 7.
