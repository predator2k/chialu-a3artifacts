## summary
The paper presents a fabricated 128-bit static end-around-carry adder for the POWER6 floating-point multiply-add pipeline. The design combines nonuniform sparse prefix computation, conditional sums, and floorplan-aware carry routing to operate beyond 5GHz at 1.1V in 65nm SOI. The paper compares the design with Ladner-Fischer and uniformly sparse Kogge-Stone implementations.

## families
### end_around_carry  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The 128-bit final adder is divided into four 32-bit groups whose carry equations wrap each group carry around to support effective subtraction. The end-around-carry block computes the four group carries while the 32-bit blocks compute conditional sums. A final transmission-gate multiplexer stage selects the sums before normalization. The carry blocks are placed so that wire delays remain balanced at each stage.

### sparse_prefix_hybrid  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The adder uses a nonuniform sparse prefix scheme selected according to physical wire length. Each 32-bit block contains an 8-bit radix-2 Kogge-Stone tree with sparsity 2 and a 32-bit radix-2 Kogge-Stone tree with sparsity 8. The trees generate carries, propagates, carry+1 information, and conditional sums. The critical carry path has fanout 1 and is replicated for intermediate carries. Both carry alternatives are propagated because this removes one stage from the critical path.

### classic_fma  (role: instantiates)
mechanism: The POWER6 datapath computes T=B+A×C in a seven-cycle multiply-add pipeline. The aligned addend and multiplier result reach a multiply counter tree, after which the proposed end-around-carry adder performs the terminal addition. Bitwise propagate/generate terms are formed at the end of cycle 3, carries and 32-bit conditional sums are computed in cycle 4, and selected sums enter normalization at the beginning of cycle 5.

## open_questions
* The paper does not identify an IEEE floating-point format, so “128-bit” may describe the internal final-adder width rather than binary128.
* The paper does not report absolute adder area, absolute active power, standalone delay, or a measured maximum frequency more precise than “beyond 5GHz.”
* The paper does not identify the multiplier reduction-tree family or the rounding mechanism used after normalization.
