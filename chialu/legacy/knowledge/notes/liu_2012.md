## summary
The paper proposes a radix-16 combined binary64 division/square-root unit that overlaps two radix-4 digit-selection stages and places speculation in a narrow path. The implementation is compared with radix-4/cascaded-radix-16 digit-recurrence units and an FMA-based Newton-Raphson implementation. The digit-recurrence unit has lower latency than the radix-4 alternative and consumes less power than the compared cascaded and Newton-Raphson solutions. (p.1059, pp.1065-1069)

## families
### digit_recurrence_sqrt_combined  (role: proposes)
mechanism: The unit decomposes each radix-16 result digit as qj = 4qHj + qLj, with qHj and qLj in {-2,-1,0,1,2}. The two radix-4 selection functions overlap by computing qL speculatively for every qH outcome. A 58-bit carry-save W-path updates the residual, while a 12-bit q-path performs selection/speculation and maintains a partial-result replica. Retiming moves result-digit completion to the cycle end and provides slack for a lower-power radix-16 CSA implementation in the lower 44 W-path bits. (pp.1060-1064)

### newton_raphson  (role: compares)
mechanism: The comparison unit augments a four-stage FMA datapath with reciprocal/reciprocal-square-root lookup tables, bypass multiplexers, and control. Division refines R[j+1] = R[j](2-R[j]d), using two FMA operations per iteration. Square root refines R[j+1] = 2^-1R[j](3-xR[j]^2), using three FMA operations per iteration. A final multiplication forms the quotient/root, and additional work computes the remainder and rounding. (pp.1067-1068)
