## summary
The paper proposes non-iterative dynamic range approximate logarithmic multipliers that apply Mitchell’s approximation to leading-one-relative truncated operands. Setting each truncated operand’s last retained bit and a post-addition compensation bit to one reduces error while narrower logarithmic converters/adders/antilogarithmic converters reduce hardware cost. Formal error analysis and machine-learning experiments assess unsigned/signed 8-, 16-, and 32-bit designs.

## families
### logarithmic  (role: extends)
mechanism: Each operand is expressed as a characteristic and fractional part after a leading-one detector. The fractional parts are truncated to t bits relative to the leading one, converted to logarithmic operands, added, compensated by one, converted back from the logarithmic representation, and shifted by the summed characteristic. The design uses Mitchell’s approximation log2(1+x)≈x. The signed variant forms approximate magnitudes with one’s complement and an OR operation before applying the unsigned DR-ALM. # pp.2-4

### dynamic_segment  (role: instantiates)
mechanism: A leading-one detector locates each operand’s most significant one. Dynamic truncation retains a fixed-width segment beginning at that one, so the processed range follows operand magnitude. The last retained bit is forced to one to compensate the multiplier’s otherwise negative error, and the retained segment feeds the logarithmic multiplication path.   # p.3
