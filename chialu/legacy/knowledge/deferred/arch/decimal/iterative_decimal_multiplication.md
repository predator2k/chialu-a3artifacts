# iterative_decimal_multiplication

Digit-by-digit decimal multiplication into a redundant accumulator.
Easy multiples of the multiplicand (2x, 4x, 5x, or 1x/2x/5x/10x from a
doubler/quintupler) are stored or generated by dedicated logic; each
multiplier digit, taken LSD first, selects the one or two multiples
whose sum is its digit product; decimal 3:2 counters and a 4:2
compressor add it into a carry-save intermediate product; one product
digit emerges per iteration, and the redundant remainder is converted
by a decimal carry-propagate adder in the final cycles. Signed-digit
recoding of the multiplier to -5..+5 shrinks the digit products to
magnitudes 2 through 5 and feeds a signed-digit accumulator instead.

The multiple set trades storage and generation logic against
operations per digit. Storing every multiple, or the tens and units
digits of every one-digit product, makes each iteration a selection
but costs registers and a generation phase. Generating 2x, 4x, and 5x by
dedicated logic takes a few gate delays, any digit product is the sum
of two of them, and the pair is absorbed by a 3:2 counter ahead of the
4:2 compressor. The doubler/quintupler alone, with add/subtract for the
remaining digits, is the smallest hardware; in abstract counts,
addition alone averages 4.5 operations per multiplier digit and
subtraction plus doubling plus quintupling brings that to 1.3.
Signed-digit recoding leaves general digit-product logic for a small
fraction of the digit pairs, but the overlapped partial product must be
recoded before the signed-digit adder and converted back to BCD at the
end.

At one digit per cycle the carry-save organization reaches a latency of
n + 4 cycles with an initiation interval of n + 1, because multiple
generation sits outside the feedback loop and the two carry-save stages
pipeline. Pairing digits halves the iteration term when a split adder
alternates multiple generation and accumulation; selective two-digit
consumption, zero skipping, early exit after the most significant
nonzero digit, and iterating over the operand with fewer significant
digits cut the average count with control logic outside the critical
loop. A linear chain of BCD adders is the simplest accumulator, decimal
compressors keep the loop short, and an overloaded-digit accumulator
that merges two multiples per cycle clocks 14% faster at 34 digits in a
0.11 um standard-cell library but its clean-up blocks cost 77% more
area than the carry-save design.

The family is fixed-iteration with data-dependent latency. It is
smaller and may clock higher than a parallel decimal multiplier, at
higher latency and lower throughput; it holds the production slot when
parallel summation fails the area and cycle-time budget, and it serves
as the engine for Newton-Raphson division and square root, where short
multiplier operands shorten the early iterations.

The family's defining structure is sequential (one or two multiplier digits per cycle into a carry-save accumulator, the rows reused over the cycles), so the library has no combinational module for it; a seed that declares it stays behavioral and the family is listed as an exception (`decimal.SEQUENTIAL_DECIMAL`).
