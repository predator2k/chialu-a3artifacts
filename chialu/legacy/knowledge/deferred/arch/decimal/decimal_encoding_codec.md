# decimal_encoding_codec

Conversion between the storage encoding of a decimal significand and the digit encoding the arithmetic consumes. Densely packed decimal (DPD) sorts each BCD digit into small (0 to 7) and large (8 or 9) and uses a Huffman-style indicator field to map three digits, 12 BCD bits, onto one 10-bit declet; one-digit and two-digit values occupy the rightmost 4 and 7 bits of the same mapping, so a field grows by zero padding. Chen-Ho is the earlier form, with 8-to-7 and 12-to-10 bit blocks. Expansion and compression are fixed bit mappings realised as a few gate levels or a lookup table. BID keeps the significand as a plain binary integer and stays in integer software. The codec is feed-forward.

significand_encoding trades storage density against the cost of reaching digits. DPD uses 1,000 of the 1,024 declet values and keeps digit boundaries, so decimal addition, shifting, rounding, and character conversion stay simple and the encoding is about 17% more compact than BCD, which fits 33 digits plus sign and a four-digit exponent into a 128-bit register that holds 32 BCD digits. DPD also leaves values 0 through 79 unchanged where Chen-Ho leaves only 0 through 7, and it matches BCD-oriented databases. Unpacked BCD needs no codec at all but costs register width, on the order of 18 extra bits per decimal64 value. BID drops the digit boundaries and does coefficient arithmetic with binary multiplication, division, shifting, tables, and corrective rounding, which is why it is the software encoding and DPD the hardware one. Packed and zoned BCD are the fixed-point relatives: arithmetic consumes packed operands, zoned serves input, editing, and output, and dedicated pack/unpack hardware converts between them.

codec_placement decides where the expansion and compression sit. At register read, each operand register carries a DPD-to-BCD expander and one BCD-to-DPD compressor precedes the result register, so the arithmetic never sees declets. Inside the operation, decode is part of the formulation stage that also extracts the exponent and special values, and encode is part of the result stage that emits the flags. Either way the conversion is cheap: roughly two gate delays or three logic levels per direction, a one-cycle milli-op on z9, and decode plus encode of about 1,900 of 113,795 area units in a 65-nm DFP fused multiply-add. What the codec does cost is cycles on every floating-point operation that packed-BCD fixed-point operations avoid, which is what the cache_decoded_operands mutation removes by storing operands internally in decoded form. The same conversion around a seed table lets the table store its address and coefficients in DPD and shrink by more than four times.

No unit template opens `decimal_misc_space`: the family belongs to a decimal floating-point unit (or a conversion between number systems) that the ALU, dot and SFU templates do not provision, so no seed declares it and the library has no module for it; the BCD ALU's decimal adders, multipliers and dividers come from `chialu/targets/rtl/families/decimal.py`.
