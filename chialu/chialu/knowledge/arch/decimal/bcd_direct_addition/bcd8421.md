# bcd8421

The 8,4,2,1 code: each digit is its 4-bit binary value, so binary
half and full adders add two digits directly and the position
corrects by 6 after a decimal carry, with a minimum of nine input
lines and five output lines per digit. A digit whose uncarried sum
is 9 passes the incoming carry through one AND and one OR instead of
four binary orders. The z900 and z10 decimal units, the 64-bit and
36-digit adders, and the converters that finish parallel decimal
multipliers all work in this code.

BCD-8421 is the pick whenever operands arrive packed from memory or
from a multiplier tree, because binary addition techniques apply
readily, redundant 4221 sum and carry digits convert into it before
the final carry-propagate adder, and the direct decimal carry logic
is derived for it under the assumption of valid digits 0 through 9.
Excess-3 is the sibling when a shared binary adder with flag-based
post-correction is preferred, at the cost of a code conversion
before and after the addition; the internal-BCD choice simplifies the
pre-correction circuit relative to that conversion.
