# separate_multiplier_and_adder

The float mode's multiply and add as two structures: the fp_adder slot's structure computes fadd and fsub with its own alignment, significand add and normalize, and the fp_multiplier slot's structure computes fmul with its own significand multiplier, each delivering an unrounded X to the mode's rounder. This is the organization of a conventional floating-point unit with distinct add and multiply pipelines, FPnew's PARALLEL slice (one `fpnew_fma` instance per format and operation group, or the dedicated `fpnew_add`/`fpnew_mul` units of its predecessors) and HardFloat's one module per (format, operation) (`AddRecFN`, `MulRecFN`). An add and a multiply can issue in the same cycle to different units, and a standalone add carries no multiplier latency; the cost is two normalizers and two exponent paths per mode, and a multiply-add sequence rounds twice.

The alternative in the same slot is one fused datapath (classic_fma and its siblings), which serves fadd as 1 * a + b and fmul as a * b + 0 through one window adder and one normalize: fewer normalizers, one rounding for a fused multiply-add, and the add pays the multiplier's window width.

A 1991 comparison of a multiply-add-chained configuration against three fused organizations, under one normalized CMOS delay and area model, puts the separate form at the least area and the shortest standalone add. The chained configuration adds an indirection path between the multiplier and the adder, and because a fused instruction need not be supported, the adder and the multiplier are separately optimized, which avoids the extra loading a fused implementation puts on them. Its hardware cost is a partial-product reduction plus 5.5 compound-adder areas plus the two units' wiring, against 7 for the fully overlapped organization and for the partially overlapped one, whose wire areas separate them, and 8.5 for the RS/6000-style unit, where the unit of area is a 53-bit compound adder. Its multiply-add latency is the sum of the multiply and the add latencies, which is the highest of those four but for the RS/6000-style unit, so the separate organization suits an instruction mix with a high proportion of standalone adds.

Two later synthesis studies measure the same split at equal technology. At 90 nm and a shared 3.2 GFlops single-precision point, a cascade of a multiplier into an adder and a fused unit reach the same 0.036 mm2/GFlops and the same 0.046 W/GFlops, with the fused unit at 10 cycles and the cascade at 12. The cascade's aligner, adder and normalizer are about 48 bits wide against about 72 bits for the fused unit, because the fused unit aligns the addend across the whole product. A TSMC 45 nm sweep of generated units separates the two by criterion: fused designs hold the throughput frontier in energy and area per operation, and cascade designs hold the latency frontier, because a dependent instruction and an add without a multiply both complete sooner. The cascade's accumulation latency is about half its multiply-add latency, while the fused unit's two latencies are equal, and keeping the multiply and the add in separate pipelines admits more aggressive clock gating.

A product FPU built this way runs the two units concurrently: the SPARC64 adder unit takes 3 cycles and its multiplier unit 4, each fully pipelined at one operation per cycle in 0.15 um six-layer-metal CMOS. The rounding contract is the visible difference. A multiply-add sequence on separate units rounds after the multiply and again after the add, which makes the intermediate result IEEE-754 compliant and the exceptions precise, and that machine exposes the sequence as two unfused instructions that route the multiplier operands to the multiply unit and the addend to the add unit over an internal bus. The fused organization instead derives its add by forcing the multiplier operand to 1.0 and its multiply by forcing the addend to 0.0, so a standalone add pays the fused unit's width: a double-precision PowerPC multiply-add-fused unit positions the addend 56 bits left of the product's binary point, shifts it over a 161-bit range around the 106-bit product, and closes with a 161-bit ones' complement addition.

## the library's module

The family has no module of its own: the seed instantiates the fp_adder's and the fp_multiplier's own family modules (`chialu/targets/rtl/families/fp.py`), each in its own unit. The fp_fma structure declares the organization; under it the fp_adder and fp_multiplier slots of the mode are active and carry their own declarations.
