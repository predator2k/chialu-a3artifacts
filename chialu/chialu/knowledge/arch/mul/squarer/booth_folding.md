# booth_folding

Both copies of the operand are expressed as radix-4 Booth digits in
{0, +-1, +-2} and the symmetry of the square folds the recoded
matrix; rearrangement leaves only one operand Booth-encoded, each
signed partial product is generated by a one's complement rather
than the two's complement a Booth multiplier needs, and one
sign-extension-prevention constant replaces per-row extension. An
8-bit square keeps 28 partial-product bits in 3 rows against 38 bits
in 5 rows for plain folding.

Above 24 bits the recoding removes about 40% of the partial products
and 46% of the matrix height relative to basic_symmetry, and a
16-bit carry-save version measured 27% less power and 26% less
delay on a 0.6 um test chip, with the Wallace-tree version gaining
less because tree delay grows only logarithmically with height. The
price is the Booth encoder and shifter-complementer, whose two
generator styles trade logic depth and glitching against
width-dependent area. Fixed-width squarers build on the same
folding and drop the low-half hardware. It is the pick when width
is moderate to large and delay or power dominate.
