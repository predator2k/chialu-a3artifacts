# reduced_precision

A narrow replica of the protected datapath computes the same operation on the operands' most-significant bits, beside the full-width unit, and a comparator flags a disagreement larger than a bound set by the replica's own truncation error. In the floating-point form the replica keeps the full sign and exponent and truncates only the mantissa; the compare tolerates a few units in the replica's last place, so the replica rounder is omitted. In the DSP form (reduced precision redundancy) the replica is a shorter-delay copy free of voltage-overscaling timing errors, and its output replaces the main output outside the maximum error-free difference. The check is feed-forward.

replica_width_bits sets the area/coverage point: an error smaller than the replica's resolution is a false negative, and a wider replica shrinks the escaping error while adding area and, because the checker is itself unprotected, more false positives. For a 32-bit floating-point adder a 4-bit checker mantissa bounds the undetected error at 25% of the result for 30% area overhead, and 14 bits reaches 0.02% at 62%. bound_type decides how the tolerance is expressed: an absolute threshold equal to the maximum error-free main/replica difference guarantees no false alarm when the main output is correct, while a relative-ulp rule maps the two-bit difference between full and truncated results onto the checker value or its neighbours before the compare. correct_by_substitution turns detection into correction: the DSP form selects the replica output outside the threshold and lets undetected errors remain as residual noise, so the contract is an application SNR rather than an exact result, and a 16-tap FIR in 0.25-µm CMOS keeps its required SNR at 60% energy saving. The floating-point checkers detect only, and a detection that misses the addition cycle holds the result uncommitted for one more cycle.

Coverage is by construction limited to large errors: a 7-bit checker mantissa on a 32-bit multiplier detects 26.5% of unmasked stuck-at faults, yet 90.8% of the escapes perturb the result by under 1%, and the combined multiplier-plus-adder checker costs 17.8% area in 45 nm. The family wins over full duplication where the application tolerates bounded error and where the replica's delay falls with width, which suits ripple-carry adders and Baugh-Wooley multipliers under voltage overscaling; it loses once the errors to be caught are small, once overscaling also corrupts the replica, or once an exact relation is required, in which case a residue channel is the substitute. Cancellation defeats the truncated prediction, so checking is gated off for unlike-sign operands whose exponents differ by zero or one, and for infinity, NaN, and denormal cases.

The generated checker (`chialu/targets/rtl/alu_checker.py`) realizes this family as a replica of replica_width_bits: the add class and mul_wide of the integer modes on the operands' top bits (the result's top bits within one replica ulp of the replica's sum, the wide product's within |a_hi| + |b_hi| + 1), fadd, fsub and fmul of the float modes on significands truncated to R bits through the engine, the result accepted within 2^(scale - R + 4) (absolute: the operands' scale, relative_ulp: the product's own scale); the bits below the replica's resolution escape by design and the fault gate holds the family to the coverage it guarantees; correct_by_substitution names a corrected output the interface lacks.
