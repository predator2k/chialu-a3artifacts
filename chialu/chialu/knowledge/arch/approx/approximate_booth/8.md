# 8

Radix-8 recoding with an approximate hard multiple: the recoder
selects six signed partial products from -4Y to 4Y for a 16x16
product, and 3Y comes from an approximate recoding adder (ARA8,
ARA8-2C or ARA8-2R) that keeps a precise upper section and
suppresses carry propagation in the lower 8 bits. A Wallace tree
reduces the products; the ABM2 variants also truncate 9 or 15 low
partial-product bits and add an average-error compensation of 1 at
bit 17 for the 15-bit truncation.

Radix-8 is the pick when fewer partial-product rows matter more than
the 3Y cost and the contract tolerates rare large errors: the
untruncated design cuts delay by nearly 20 percent against the
accurate radix-8 multiplier (2.38 against 2.99 ns in STM 28 nm) at
an NMED of 1.92e-5 and a 99.77 percent probability of relative error
below 2 percent, and the truncated variants cut area by about 18 and
43 percent. Errors from the approximate 3Y can be large when they
enter a significant partial product, and a 30-tap FIR loses about
3 dB of output SNR. Radix-4 is the sibling when error must stay
small, and hybrid high radix when accurate MSB encoding is required.
