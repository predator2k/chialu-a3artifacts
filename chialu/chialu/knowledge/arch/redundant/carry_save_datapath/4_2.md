# 4_2

The 4:2 compressor as the reduction primitive: each cell takes four
vectors and emits a sum and a carry, with a carry-out that depends on
the cell's inputs rather than its carry-in, so no carry ripples between
bit positions inside a level. In an iterative accumulator two inputs
take the shifted sum/carry of the previous step and two the current
tree outputs, and in a merging datapath the four source operands enter
one cell ahead of a single completion adder.

This is the pick when operands arrive in pairs or the tree must be
shallow and regular: the SPIM accumulator runs at almost twice the
speed of a conventional partially pipelined array for roughly equal
hardware, the Pentium 4 address generator merges four operands into one
4:2 stage before a sparse-tree completion adder, and DSP-block
sum-of-products chains keep every intermediate in 4:2 form until the
block exit. A floating-point multiply-accumulator holds its
accumulation in a 4:2 array so that the carry-propagate adder leaves the
loop, and assimilates once after the loop with the carry vector shifted
left by one bit. The cell is slower than a full adder per level, and a third
compressor stage in a four-product 9-bit MAC failed a 600 MHz
constraint, so the products were split into two pairwise sums; the 3:2
sibling keeps the cells smaller at more levels.

The library's carry-save adder realizes this compressor as two 3:2 levels with the fourth input zero in a single op (`chialu/targets/rtl/families/redundant.py`).
