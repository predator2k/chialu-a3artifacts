# 3_2

The full-adder cell as the reduction primitive: each level takes three
vectors, the two words of the redundant state and one new operand, and
emits a sum word and a carry word shifted one position, so a level
costs one full-adder delay whatever the width and the carry stored in
the carry word never exceeds one. Cascaded levels form the carry-save
array or the Wallace tree; the same cell holds a partial product, an
SRT partial remainder or a running accumulation between iterations.

This is the cell of nearly every reported carry-save chain, from the
360/91 multiplier tree and the Pentium radix-4 divider to on-line
recurrences and modular end-around trees, and it is the pick when the
chain adds one operand per step or the cells must stay small and
identical. Its price is depth: a level removes only one vector, so a
tree over many operands needs more levels than a 4:2 tree, and on an
FPGA a chain of full adders is left to the carry logic in k-bit chunks,
with k=1 the plain carry-save form and k=32 reaching 400 MHz. The 4:2
sibling halves the level count where the operands arrive in pairs.

The library's carry-save adder realizes this compressor as one full-adder level over the two operands and the carry-in word (`chialu/targets/rtl/families/redundant.py`).
